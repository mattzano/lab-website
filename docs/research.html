Our research

We follow disease along the motor unit, from the motor neuron to the muscle fibre, and move between the clinic, the bench and large datasets. Five lines run through our work.

01

SBMA

Kennedy’s disease: natural history, outcome measures and trials

Spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy is caused by a CAG repeat expansion in the androgen receptor gene. We follow people with SBMA over many years to describe how the disease progresses, and to make trial endpoints meaningful for patients.

We have estimated the smallest changes in function that patients perceive, measured fatigability with the six-minute walk test, validated outcome scales and studied blood markers such as creatinine. We also coordinate the clenbuterol trial in SBMA.

Selected papers

  • Longitudinal progression of 6-minute walk test-derived fatigability in spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy: a retrospective study. Muscle Nerve, 2026
  • Estimating the minimal clinically important difference of functional outcomes in spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy. J Neurol, 2026
  • Functional outcome measures to optimize drug development in spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy: results from a meta-analysis of the global SBMA dataset. Neurology, 2024
  • The value of serum creatinine as biomarker of disease progression in spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy (SBMA). Sci Rep, 2023

02

ALS biomarkers

Measuring and stratifying amyotrophic lateral sclerosis

ALS varies widely from person to person. We combine neurophysiology (cortical excitability, motor evoked potentials), brain imaging (MRI and [18F]FDG-PET) and fluid biomarkers (neurofilaments, chitinases) to measure upper and lower motor neuron involvement, predict progression and stratify patients.

We also follow biomarkers in people receiving new treatments, such as tofersen in SOD1-ALS, and take part in phase 2 and 3 clinical trials.

Selected papers

  • Cortical excitability as a prognostic and phenotypic stratification biomarker in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. Ann Neurol, 2025
  • Metabolic and fluid biomarkers support microglia activation in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. Ann Clin Transl Neurol, 2026
  • MEPs and MRI motor band sign as potential complementary markers of upper motor neuron involvement in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. Eur J Neurol, 2025
  • Neurodegenerative and neuroinflammatory changes in SOD1-ALS patients receiving tofersen. Sci Rep, 2025

03

TDP-43 and RNA

Cryptic splicing, biomarkers and immunity

In almost all people with ALS, the RNA-binding protein TDP-43 leaves the nucleus of affected cells. Without it, cells include cryptic exons and cryptic polyadenylation sites in their RNAs, which switch off genes such as UNC13A and STMN2.

With colleagues at UCL Queen Square, we map these events, ask which cryptic exons can be measured as biomarkers in patients, and test splice-correcting therapies. Cryptic peptides can also be recognised by CD8 T cells, a link between TDP-43 pathology and adaptive immunity that we are now studying in Padova.

Selected papers

  • TDP-43 loss and ALS-risk SNPs drive mis-splicing and depletion of UNC13A. Nature, 2022
  • UNC13A splice correction rescues synaptic dysfunction induced by TDP-43 loss. Nature, 2026
  • TDP-43 loss induces cryptic polyadenylation in ALS/FTD. Nat Neurosci, 2025
  • TDP-43 pathology induces CD8+ T cell activation through cryptic epitope recognition. bioRxiv, 2025

04

Muscle and junction

Skeletal muscle and the neuromuscular junction in motor neuron disease

Muscle is more than a passive victim of motor neuron loss. Using patient muscle biopsies and disease models, we study muscle regeneration, innervation and the neuromuscular junction in ALS and SBMA.

At the bench we are studying phosphorylated tau in ALS skeletal muscle and developing ELISA assays for new muscle and blood biomarkers. In 2026 we co-organised the international conference Skeletal Muscle in ALS in Padova.

Selected papers

  • Sympathetic neuropathology is revealed in muscles affected by amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. Front Physiol, 2023
  • Immune-mediated myogenesis and acetylcholine receptor clustering promote a slow disease progression in ALS mouse models. Inflamm Regen, 2023
  • AR cooperates with SMAD4 to maintain skeletal muscle homeostasis. Acta Neuropathol, 2022

05

Genetics

Repeat expansions and risk variants across populations

Population-scale genomic datasets, such as the UK Biobank, show that pathogenic repeat expansions and risk variants for neuromuscular disease are more common than diagnosed cases would suggest. The AR CAG expansion behind SBMA and pathogenic SOD1 variants are two examples.

We use these data to estimate how many people carry disease-causing variants, to study what modifies their effect, and to plan for earlier diagnosis and intervention.

Selected papers

  • Unexpected frequency of the pathogenic AR CAG repeat expansion in the general population. Brain, 2023
  • Increased frequency of repeat expansion mutations across different populations. Nat Med, 2024
  • High prevalence of SOD1 pathogenic variants in the UK Biobank: implications for early intervention in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. Ann Neurol, 2026
  • Common and rare variant association analyses in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis identify 15 risk loci with distinct genetic architectures and neuron-specific biology. Nat Genet, 2021
